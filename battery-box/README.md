# 4Runner Electronics Card Cage

**Vehicle:** Toyota 4Runner, Goose Gear plate system
**Replaces:** the plywood battery box that sits on the plate against the IceBox 1.3 slotted back panel
**Status (2026-10-18): v12s is the build** (620 × 487 × 282 mm, 24.4 × 19.2 × 11.1 in: the 12/1200-class inverter and two SuperPack 60Ah in the v13 interior, all four panels and the cubes from the second IceBox; the MultiPlus-II order was cancelled, so v12 and v13 below are reference only). Previously: v12 was the build (IceBox footprint, 11.1 in tall, not card based: v13's interior with the MultiPlus-II 12/3000 ordered 2026-10-14 and the two SuperPack 60Ah owned today; the batteries upgrade to v13's two Smart 200Ah later by reprinting the two trays; see [Versions](#versions) and its [printing notes](#v12-printing-notes)). v11 (17.25 in deep, card based) is the runner-up; v9/v10 and the earlier cube/bookshelf versions are kept for reference. The 4 mod kits were returned; the frame comes from one second IceBox 1.3. Cargo-ceiling height still to measure.

**Goal:** a cube built from **12 uncut Goose Gear mod-kit bars**. Each bar is the IceBox 1.3 width rail (~437 mm), so the frame is 487.4 mm in every direction: the IceBox's exact width, 71 mm shorter than the IceBox, standing against its slotted back panel. It holds **four SuperPacks** (three upright, one on its long side above them), and everything else is on cards that drop in from the top, with the distribution card right behind the front panel so the output runs are short. Same look (1" extrusion frame, black corner cubes, black panels), 3D-printed interior, **no off-the-shelf extrusion and no saw cuts**.

## Versions

**v9 (kept for reference): v8 plus a fixed bus deck, so every card unplugs and lifts out alone** (`python cage.py v9`)

The problem with v8: the battery leads run under card 3, through card 2, up to the bus bars on card 1, so no card comes out without its neighbours. v9 moves everything that is wired to more than one card off the cards:

- **Bus deck.** A printed 6 mm plate bolted to the tops of the battery rack legs, at 468 mm, 91 mm under the lid. It carries both bus bars, the two battery breakers, the SmartShunt, and the mating halves of every card's connector along its front edge: an **SB175** for the inverter and three **SB50s** for the MPPT, the Orion and the Garmin/Cerbo feed. A hand window in the plate reaches the battery lugs below it.
- **Two cards, one plug each side.** The inverter card carries the MultiPlus 12/1200 and a single SB175 receptacle at the top of its rear face. The charge card carries the MPPT (at the top), the Orion and the Cerbo on its front face and five SB50 receptacles at the top of its rear face (MPPT, Orion, Cerbo, solar in, alternator in). The deck has **no fixed connector halves**: every pigtail is lugged to a bus bar or breaker on the deck and plugs into the card at its far end. The five charge-card pigtails run forward in the 18 mm chase between the outboard rail and the guide posts, so the inverter card lifts out without touching them.
- **Service:** lid off, unplug that card at the deck, lift it. Batteries: unplug both cards, unbolt the deck, lift it, lift the batteries. Nothing on any card is wired to another card.
- **Front panel:** shore-power inlet and inverter outlet (L14-30), the Anderson inlets for the alternator feed and the solar array, and the 12 V sockets in the lower band. All of those are plugs too, so the front panel comes off clean.
- Depth: **20 mm between the deck's front edge and the inverter card** (just the card's SB175 housing plus 4 mm; the 4 AWG pigtail rises out of its flying half and bends back over the deck inside the 60 mm lid room), **22 mm behind the charge card** for its rear-face SB50s, **4 mm** behind the charge card's parts and **10 mm between the MPPT and the front panel**. The MPPT, the deepest unit at 70 mm, sits at the **top** of the charge card; the Orion and the Cerbo (turned 90°) fill the middle band; the bottom band of the card is empty, and that is where every panel-mounted connector lives: the five 12 V / USB sockets, the L14-30 shore inlet and inverter outlet, and the two SB50 inlets for solar and alternator, all with 90 mm of depth behind them. The Garmin PowerSwitch lies on the **deck**, where the SB50 row used to be, fed by a 150 mm lead from the positive bus bar; its four outputs leave through a PP45 block at the deck's front edge and the socket harness runs down the outboard chase to the front panel. **568 × 353 × 559 mm (22.4 in deep), 7.6 in shorter and 5.3 in narrower than the IceBox**, same height. ~58 kg.

**Connectors.** The v9 cards are **cut down 50 mm (444 tall)** so the connector stack at the top of each card leaves **60 mm under the lid** for the pigtail cables, which leave the mated halves upward. Every card carries its **own fixed Anderson halves at the top of its rear face, standing with the mating face up**: one SB175 on the inverter card; five SB50s in a row on the charge card for the MPPT, the Orion, the Garmin/Cerbo feed, and the solar and alternator inlets. Everything below the connectors is free for the wire bends up to them. The bus deck is extended 40 mm forward over the wiring gap and carries the other fixed halves, **pointing at the cards** with proper spacing. Short pigtails with a flying half at each end join deck to card. To pull a card: lift the pigtail halves straight up off the card's connectors (25 mm for the SB175, 15 mm for an SB50), then lift the card. The deck ends come off forward on the extension if a pigtail ever needs replacing. All of it is reached from above with the lid off; the housings are Anderson's own STEP models.

**Garmin outputs.** The PowerSwitch's four socket circuits leave the charge card through an **Anderson Powerpole PP45 block** (8 poles, 10 AWG contacts, 30 A per Garmin channel) at the top of the card's front face, mating face up like the rest. The harness from the front-panel sockets ends in the mating block; unclip it before the card comes out.

**Fastener rule: no loose nuts inside the box.** Nothing is tightened blind. Every fastener in the assembled box goes into a heat-set brass insert in the printed part, a 1/4-20 T-nut slid into a rail slot, or self-forms a thread in plastic (the shelf screws). The only nuts anywhere are on sub-assemblies that are built on the bench with both sides in hand and then dropped in: the MultiPlus's four M6 through-bolts and the card tiles' lap bolts, on the card before it goes in the box; and the panel-mount ports (L14-30 screws, the sockets' threaded rings), on the front panel before it goes on.

**Battery rack: how every part connects.** The rack is a closed load path from the frame back to the frame, so the box lifts as one unit and nothing relies on the Goose Gear plate:

```
frame bottom rails ──T-nuts──▶ tray L tabs ──▶ trays ──M4 insert──▶ leg foot tabs ──▶ lower legs
                                 │                                         │ half-lap + shelf M4
                                 ▼                                         ▼
                      lower batteries (strap)                  shelves ──▶ upper batteries (strap)
                                                                           │
                                                   upper legs ──M4──▶ bus deck ──M4──▶ deck ties ──T-nut──▶ frame corner bars
```

Under 1 g braking each stack puts about 15 kg sideways into the tray lip and tabs, and the upper battery 7.5 kg into the shelf lip and its four screws. The legs are 12 mm square and only ever see the deck's weight in compression, the shelf's screws in shear and the strap and battery friction.

Print the rack in ASA rather than PETG: a closed box in a parked truck exceeds PETG's softening range in summer.

**Assembly, in order**

1. **Frame.** IceBox height and depth rails (depth rails cut to 606), kit-bar width rails cut to 294, printed corner cubes with 1/4-20 end-tap screws. Floor panel over the card zone only. Side, back and front panels screwed to the outside of the bars with T-nuts; the front panel gets the socket insert, the two SB50 inlets and the L14-30 inlet and outlet before it goes on.
2. **Battery rack.** Press the inserts: 4 × M4 in each tray's corner pads, 4 × M5 in its back pads, 1 × M4 in each upper leg's deck tab. Trays in, side tabs bolted down into the depth rails, back brackets to the tray pads and the back rail (T-nuts slid in before the corner cubes go on); the 4 × M6 into the plate only if you want them. Lower legs: stand each in its corner, foot tab onto the wall top, M4 into the pad. Lay each lower strap in its tray channel, battery in, strap over the top, buckle at the front. Shelves on: upper legs onto the laps, then one M4 through each shelf end wall into the lap. Strap in the shelf channel, upper battery in, strap over, buckle at the front. Battery leads (6 AWG, M6 lugs) left loose on top.
3. **Bus deck.** Both halves splined together on the bench; bus bars, breakers, shunt, the Garmin PowerSwitch and its PP45 block screwed on with inserts; the six pigtails (SB175 for the inverter; SB50s for MPPT, Orion and Cerbo; PP45 socket harness) lugged to the bus bars and breakers, flying halves at their free ends. Deck onto the leg boss tabs, 8 × M4. Deck ties: T-nut into each back corner bar, 2 × M4 down through the deck. Battery leads up through the hand windows to the breakers and bus bars.
4. **Guide posts.** Four posts, two segments each; the bottom foot sits on the depth rail beside the floor panel and bolts into its T-slot, the top foot bolts up into the top rail.
5. **Each card on the bench.** Lap the two tiles (lower tile's rabbet to the front, upper's to the back), glue, and bolt the lap with 3 × M4 countersunk from the front with nyloc nuts behind (the card is on the bench, both sides in hand). Press in the inserts: inverter card M6 for the SB175; charge card M4/M5/M6 per unit plus the SB50 row and the PP45. Mount the units: MultiPlus on its feet with the four M6 through-bolts, fender washers and nyloc nuts on the back; MPPT, Orion, Cerbo, Garmin on their inserts. Wire every unit to the card's own connectors at the top with the sizes in the table above, and label each lead. Test the card's internal wiring before it goes in.
6. **Install.** Drop the inverter card into its posts, then the charge card. Plug each pigtail's flying half down onto the card's receptacles: the SB175 straight off the deck for the inverter card; the charge-card SB50s via the outboard chase. Connect the front-panel harnesses: inlet pigtails to the charge card's two inlet SB50s, the socket harness to the PP45, AC cords to the MultiPlus. Lid on.
7. **Pull a card later:** lid off, lift that card's pigtail halves and harness block straight up, lift the card. Nothing else moves.

**v12 (the build): 11.1 in tall IceBox footprint, MultiPlus-II with two SuperPack 60Ah, batteries upgradeable to v13** (`python cage.py v12`, viewer "v12")

v5's idea (a second IceBox 1.3 frame, same width and depth as the IceBox) cut down to **762 × 487 × 282 mm, 11.1 in tall**, 10.9 in below the IceBox deck. The parts in it only need 254 (the MultiPlus on its long side, 249 mm plus its plate; the top rails do not limit it because they only run along the edges), but the box is cut at **v13's height** so that the same frame, panels, lid, posts, clips and port strip carry a MultiPlus-II and two Smart 200Ah later (see v13 and the upgrade path below). The same principles carry over: printed trays tabbed to the rails so the box lifts as a unit, the Blue Sea 7148 breakers, 30 mm of lug room, inserts everywhere and no loose nuts inside, every print under 256 mm.

**Interior = v13's.** v12 is v13's interior with the **MultiPlus-II 12/3000/120-50** (ordered 2026-10-14) on its long side along the inboard side, hanging on the full-depth plate by its hook and holes (no shims: the plate carries it, which is why the plate is bolted to the shelf wall and held in two posts), and **two SuperPack 60Ah** (owned today) in v13's outboard zone, one tray at each end so both rail brackets reach their rails (the trays are 167 wide like v13's, with a solid 21 mm inboard wall carrying three M4 inserts; the shelf's lip bolts to them from the column side, heads reachable from above, placed where no breaker sits over them), and v13's bus shelf, column units, cable window, front panel and lid unchanged. The v13 zone table below applies. The same box with the MultiPlus 12/1200 is `python cage.py v12_1200` (the 12/1200 hangs 22 mm up so its lower keyholes reach the plate); the earlier 2 × 2 four-battery interior is `v12_4batt`.

**Phase-1 limit:** two SuperPack 60Ah are 120 Ah and **60 A continuous** (160 A for 10 s), so the MultiPlus-II can only deliver about 700 W of AC continuously until the batteries are upgraded; the SuperPacks' own BMSs cut out above that. Set the II's charger to **60 A** (two 30 A charge limits) in VEConfigure, and remember its 13 W idle is about 1 Ah per hour from a 120 Ah bank. Build the DC side for v13 now (2/0 leads, 400 A class T fuse, 250 A-class bus bars and breakers) so the battery upgrade touches nothing but the trays.

**Rail slots.** The IceBox's panels are captured in the rails' centreline slots, so the top and bottom slots of every rail are taken and only the inner-face slot is free. Every tie in v12 is therefore a horizontal 1/4-20 into a rail's inner slot: countersunk through the outboard trays' outer walls and the bus shelf's outer wall with the heads flush inside, through the posts' solid sills, and through printed clips on the posts' tops into the top rails. The panels sit inside the bar envelope, 3 mm into each slot. Panel-mounted units hang on 6 mm printed mounts screwed through the panel from outside into inserts, and are fed by SB50 pigtails, so a panel unbolts complete with its units. The MultiPlus on its long side is allowed by Victron with the terminals facing down or sideways; here they face the column. The frame is one IceBox 1.3: width and depth rails uncut, height rails cut to 231, leaving four 277 mm offcuts.

**Upgrade path to v13** (the 1500 W AC load). v12 and v13 are the same box, the same interior and the same inverter. To upgrade: lift out the two SuperPacks and their trays; print v13's two split trays (about 1 kg of filament); fit the two Smart 200Ah with their VE.Bus BMS and raise the II's charger from 60 A to its 120 A maximum (the pair would take 200). Nothing else changes.

**From the second IceBox, not printed.** The 8 corner cubes and 12 rails (height rails cut to 231), the two side panels cut down to 237 tall, the **perforated rear panel cut down** to be the box's back panel (against the IceBox), and the top panel uncut as the lid. In v12s the **front panel is the solid offcut of a side panel** (762 × 277 is left after each side is cut to 237; the front needs 443 × 237) with the ports cut straight through it, so nothing on the box is printed sheet. In v12 and v13 the **front panel is still drawn printed**: two tiles 231 × 237 × 6 on a vertical 20 mm half-lap at mid-width with three M4 through it (bench), sliding into the rails' slots like the sheets.

<a id="rail-cutting"></a>
**Cutting and tapping the rails, one shot** (2026-10-17). The frame rails come from the second IceBox already end-tapped for the corner-cube bolts; a cut rail needs its **new end** tapped. Cut only one end of each rail and keep the factory end.

| Box | Height rails ×4 | Depth rails ×4 | Width rails ×4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| v12 (762 deep, the build) | 508.0 → **231.2** (offcut 276.8 each) | 711.2 uncut | 436.6 uncut |
| v12s (620 deep) | 508.0 → **231.2** | 711.2 → **569.2** (offcut 142.0) | 436.6 uncut |

Those lengths assume 25.4 mm corner cubes with the rails butting the cube faces. **Before cutting anything, measure an uncut width rail**: it should be 436.6. If it is not, the cube is not 25.4 and every cut length shifts by the same amount; tell the model (`C`) and re-read the table.

1. **Order the cuts if you can.** An 80/20 distributor or a machine shop will cut to ±0.1 mm and end-tap 1/4-20 for a few dollars a rail. That is the only way the first shot is guaranteed. If you cut at home, the rest of this list is how to make it survivable.
2. **Practice on what you cannot ruin.** The four height-rail offcuts are 276.8 long: tap those ends first. Each 508 height rail also holds a second 231.2 if the first cut is bad, so cut the height rails before the depth rails and treat them as practice. A 711 depth rail gives one 569 and no second chance, so those are last, after the saw and the tap are proven.
3. **Cut square and slightly long.** Mitre saw with a non-ferrous carbide blade (80 to 100 teeth, blade waxed) or a bandsaw, rail clamped, a stop block so all four are identical, cut at 569.7 or 231.7. Then face the end to length on a disc sander or with a mill file, checking with a square in both directions and a caliper at all four corners. Deburr inside the bore and the slots.
**Tapping tools to buy** (one size, 1/4-20; not a tap-and-die set, whose carbon-steel plug tap and loose handle are how a bore gets cross-threaded):

| Item | What to get | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tap | 1/4-20 **HSS spiral-point** ("gun") tap: Irwin Hanson HSS, Vermont American HSS, Drill America or OSG | pushes chips ahead of itself in a deep bore; carbon steel and plug taps do not |
| Handle | T-handle tap wrench sized for 1/4 in taps | the ratcheting handle from a kit wobbles |
| Guide | tap guide block with a 1/4 in hole (Big Gator or any aluminium tapping block) | sits on the rail end and holds the tap square for the first threads; the piece that makes it a one-shot job |
| Fluid | Tap Magic Aluminum or WD-40 Specialist cutting oil | aluminium galls a dry tap |
| Drill | #7 (5.1 mm) bit, **only if** a caliper shows the bore under 5.0 mm | the 10-series bore is normally the tap-drill size already |
| Checks | caliper, small machinist's square | squareness of the cut end before tapping |

4. **Tap, don't drill.** The 10-series bore is 0.20 in, which is the 1/4-20 tap drill, so no drilling; if a caliper shows the bore under 5.0 mm, run a #7 drill (5.1 mm) through first. Use a spiral-point (gun) tap for aluminium, a tap guide block so it starts square, tapping fluid, a quarter turn back every half turn, and chips blown out twice. Tap 30 mm deep, more than the cube bolt needs. A plug tap in a hand chuck without a guide is how threads get cross-cut.
5. **Dry-fit before the panels.** Bolt the four cut rails into their cubes with no panels, check the diagonals of each face match within 1 mm, then take it apart and build it properly with the panels in the slots.

**One thing to decide before the saw touches a depth rail:** v12s cuts them to 569. That is permanent, and the MultiPlus-II you ordered is 578 long and will never fit a 620 box. Cut the depth rails only if the 12/1200 stays for good; the v12 build at 762 needs no depth cut at all.

### Orientation notes
- **Orion XS and MPPT 100/30:** both upright on the upper card with terminals down, exactly as Victron asks (the vertical layout is what made this possible).

- **SuperPacks:** three upright with 14 mm of lug clearance under the shelf; the fourth lies on its long side on a shelf that supports the whole face, which is what Victron asks for. The stack is 379 mm of 431. The top battery comes out first and exposes the lugs of the batteries under it.

### Inverter sizing against four SuperPack 60Ah

| | DC draw at full output | Bank limit (4 × SuperPack 60Ah) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| MultiPlus 12/800/35 (today) | ~70 A | 120 A continuous | fine, with 40 % headroom |
| MultiPlus 12/1200/50 | ~105 A | 120 A | ok, little headroom |
| MultiPlus-II 12/3000/120-50 | ~220 A (2400 W ÷ 12.8 V ÷ 0.93) | 120 A continuous, 320 A for 10 s | **no**: the BMSs cut out above ~1.4 kW of AC load. Its 120 A charger is exactly the bank's 30 A × 4 limit. |
| MultiPlus-II 12/4k/160-50 | ~310 A | same | **no**, and its 160 A charger must be capped at 120 A |

The number that matters is 30 A per battery continuous (Victron datasheet). Four in parallel is 120 A, which is about 1.5 kW. To actually run a 3 kVA inverter you need 220 A: eight 60Ah SuperPacks, or three 100Ah SuperPacks (100 A each), or a different battery. Wiring and bus bars would also move from 6 AWG / 150 A to 2/0 or 4/0 and a 300–400 A class T fuse.

**Physically** the MultiPlus-II does not fit a 437 mm card either way: 578 mm (12/3000) or 631 mm (12/4k) on the long axis, against 437 inside and 502 tall (`python cage.py icebox --inverter=multiplus_ii` prints the fit failure). The model keeps the 12/800.
